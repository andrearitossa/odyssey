# Short-form story engagement research

Research was collected 29 September 2026. Platform material concerns short-form video advertising rather than interactive fiction; it is used for attention and measurement principles, not as evidence that a text story should imitate an ad.

## Findings and product implications

1. **Put the specific promise in the first beat, then fulfill it.** YouTube says viewers decide in the initial seconds, recommends that the introduction immediately deliver on the title/thumbnail promise, and asks creators to use retention at key moments to understand reactions. TikTok similarly reports that more than 63% of its highest-CTR videos show their key message/product within the first three seconds and says it should be embedded in an expected narrative. For Odyssey, the opening should name a concrete jeopardy plus a personal objective in its first sentence; the first choice must act on that promise. Do not front-load lore.
   - [YouTube: performance and recommendations](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en)
   - [TikTok: Auction Ads Creative Tips (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf)

2. **Build anticipation after the hook, then inspect where it fails.** YouTube’s audience-retention guidance identifies dips as abandonment/skipping and suggests bringing later compelling moments earlier when they retain viewers. This supports a compact scene shape: immediate disturbance → one revealing or consequential beat → decision. Instrument story start, first action, each completed turn, custom-action use, and return/resume; compare turn-to-turn continuation rather than declaring a universal word-count target.
   - [YouTube: audience-retention key moments](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)

3. **Choices need visibly different situational outcomes.** In an experiment with 88 participants, Cardona-Rivera et al. found higher reported agency when choices differed in situational content. The relevant product standard is not merely three differently worded verbs; each option should alter at least one durable element: location/access, time/threat, resource, relationship, or obligation. The next scene should explicitly show the chosen effect.
   - [Cardona-Rivera et al., “Foreseeing Meaningful Choices” (AIIDE 2014)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12716)

4. **Perceived contingency supports transportation and return intent.** An online experiment (N=134) found that interactive storytelling increased perceived contingency, which mediated narrative transportation; transportation in turn related to higher intention to revisit. Odyssey should therefore make causality observable: after a turn, name what changed because of the player’s action before presenting the next pressure.
   - [Chung, “How Interactive Storytelling Persuades”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/08838151.2020.1848180)

5. **Agency can improve connection; credible world response is the product inference.** Dillman, Rogers, and Barnard’s experiments found that giving agency over a character increased user-character connection. The conclusion that Odyssey must preserve established facts is a design inference from that result and the product’s free-text promise: treat unsupported custom-action premises as intent to negotiate, search for, or attempt something—not silently create the missing key, person, or exit.
   - [Dillman et al., “Eliciting Behavior From Interactive Narratives”](https://digitalcommons.butler.edu/ccom_papers/148/)

## Guardrails for applying this research

- “First seconds” translates to the first sentence and first action in text, not to a claim that all story turns must be shorter.
- Optimize sustained, voluntary continuation and return, not only the next tap. Fast hooks that erase continuity create a short-term click but reduce trust.
- Test promise–opening pairs and choice consequence cues with real retention/return data. Platform advertising benchmarks are not a substitute for Odyssey’s product metrics.
